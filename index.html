How a day goes

Motor out. Fill the hold. Come home.

The left joystick steers. The buttons on the right cast, dock, light the lamp and reel. One finger orbits the camera. Two fingers pan and pinch to zoom. A keyboard works alongside the buttons.

01

Set sail

The sampan has an outboard and a tiller. Open the throttle in open water, and ease off among the moored boats.

02

Fish

Cast the net and wait for the haul. Or work the line: strike on the bite, and ease the reel when the nylon runs hot.

03

Sell

Dock and walk ashore. The fish market buys the catch. The grocer sells rice, kerosene, carbide and ice.

04

Shelter

Listen to the radio. A Signal No. 8 in open water works on the hull. Come in before the sea does.

Victoria Harbour and beyond

Four towns. One tide table.

Home is the Aberdeen typhoon shelter, with floating restaurants and sampans at the pier. The chart reaches Yau Ma Tei, Cheung Chau and Sai Kung. Ferries and motor junks keep their own routes. Lighthouses mark the channels after dark.

Squid and cuttlefish come to a carbide lamp after dark. Ice in the hold keeps an overnight catch from turning. Rice and kerosene are for the family stove. Run out, and the next dawn is harder.

The same water, three lights

Day, dusk, and the neon hour.

The harbour follows the clock. Afternoon is bright along the shore. Dusk turns the water copper. By evening the sea is dark and the clouds close in.

Ashore

Dock, and the harbour becomes a town.

Stepping off the boat switches to a walkable street: tong lau, balconies, laundry poles and neon blades. Push the joystick to walk. Press A to talk or to step up to a counter. Time keeps running, and rain falls over the roofs.

Fish market

Sell the catch across the counter. Prices follow the fish and the day.

Grocer

Rice, kerosene, carbide and ice. The four things a boat cannot skip.

Trading company

Plastic flowers, garments, radios, canned food, shrimp paste and dried seafood.

Boatyard

Repairs, and upgrades: a nylon net, a reel, a larger ice hold, a stronger outboard.

Temple and the night aboard

A fortune stick, then dinner on deck.

At the temple, pay for incense, bow three times and shake the cylinder. One stick a day. A strong lot lends a few hours of fishing luck.

Back at your boat you can rest until dawn: the radio, the net to mend, a story for the children, the boat altar, then sleep. You wake ready to fish again.
